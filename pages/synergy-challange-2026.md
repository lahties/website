In 2006, [MySpace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myspace) was the most visited website in the US - bigger than Google, valued at $12 billion, home to extreme custom profiles, autoplaying music, and bands like Arctic Monkeys who built careers there before ever signing a label. Then News Corp bought it, chased ad revenue, product development slowed and Facebook steadily overtook it. By 2011, it was sold just for $35 million.

So, here is your challenge:

<h2 style="margin-top: 0">Bring MySpace back for 2026, without losing what made it MySpace.</h2>

MySpace's identity was radical self-expression and a direct line between musicians and fans - a niche nobody really owns today (Instagram is curated, TikTok is algorithm-first, nobody lets you customize a profile with your own code anymore). Your solution needs to answer:

- **Niche:** who's this for, and why would they pick it over Instagram, TikTok, Discord or others?
- **Identity:** how are you retaining the identity of the original app?
- **Money:** how does it make money without repeating the same mistakes?

## Deliverable

- A 5-minute pitch covering the niche, the product concept, the business model, and go-to-market.
- Slides for the pitch sent to [huba.nagy@lahties.com](mailto:huba.nagy@lahties.com) by 13:00 on the 16th of September, 2026

## Judged on

| Criteria | What we're looking for |
|---|---|
| Market feasibility | A real, sizeable audience |
| Identity fit | Feels like revived MySpace, not a generic social app |
| Innovation | A genuinely fresh idea, not "MySpace but with AI" |
